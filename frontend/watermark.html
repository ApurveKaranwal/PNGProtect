About Invisible Watermarking

What is it?

Invisible watermarking is the process of embedding information into digital content (like images) in a way that is not visible to the human eye but can be detected by computer algorithms.

How it works (LSB Steganography)

Our method utilizes Least Significant Bit (LSB) Steganography. Digital images are made of pixels, and each pixel has color values (Red, Green, Blue). Changing the very last bit of these values (the "least significant" one) changes the color so slightly that human eyes cannot notice the difference.

However, we can use these bits to store data—like your signature, copyright info, or a unique ID.

Why use it?

  • Provenance: Prove you are the original creator of an artwork.
  • Copyright Protection: distinct from visible signatures that can be cropped out, invisible watermarks persist across the entire image.
  • Leak Tracking: If you share sensitive images privately, different watermarks for different recipients can help track the source of a leak.
Did you know?

Even major AI companies use similar techniques to "mark" AI-generated images to distinguish them from human-created art.
